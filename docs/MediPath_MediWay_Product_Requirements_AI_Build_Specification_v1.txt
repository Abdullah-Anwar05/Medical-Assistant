How the AI development agent must use this document
Treat this document as the consolidated source of product intent and functional requirements. Do not assume that any third-party integration, clinical data feed, insurer API, hospital booking connection, AI diagnostic capability or live waiting-time feed already exists. Build modular interfaces and clearly labelled simulated data until contracts, credentials, data permissions, clinical validation and technical specifications are supplied.
Where a requirement is ambiguous, preserve it as a configurable workflow and flag it for product-owner, clinical, legal or insurer confirmation. Never invent clinical thresholds, insurer rules, surgeon outcome statistics, hospital availability or authorisation decisions.

1. Executive summary
MediPath and MediWay are two connected products forming one digital ecosystem for elective surgical care. MediPath is the patient-facing mobile application and responsive patient website. MediWay is the secure browser-based workspace for hospitals, consultants, secretaries, referral coordinators, insurance administrators and authorised operational staff.
The platform is intended to guide a patient from initial symptoms and diagnosis through specialist discovery, clinical assessment, insurance eligibility and pre-authorisation (where relevant), funding clearance, booking, surgery, rehabilitation and outcome collection. The first release should focus on orthopaedic pathways, especially total knee replacement for osteoarthritis, then expand to hip replacement and other elective specialties.
1.1 Product vision
“From diagnosis to movement. One connected journey.” Empower patients to make informed treatment choices, reduce fragmented administration, improve the completeness of referrals and funding submissions, and give care teams a coordinated view of the patient pathway.
1.2 Product principles
Patient-centred, accessible, transparent and mobile-first.
Clinical safety before automation or commercial optimisation.
Separate clinical suitability, insurer eligibility, insurer authorisation, financial clearance and confirmed booking statuses.
Show provenance, date, context, limitations and denominators for every outcome statistic.
Do not rank clinicians using paid placement or unadjusted misleading metrics.
Use AI as decision support with accountable human review for consequential clinical decisions.
Build an integration-ready architecture; never represent mock data as live data.

3. Product boundaries and delivery assumptions
3.1 In scope
Patient registration, identity, consent and profile management.
Medical history, symptoms, pain/function questionnaires, medications, allergies, comorbidities and previous treatment.
Upload and management of X-rays, MRI, CT, reports, referral letters and relevant clinical documents.
Procedure-specific discovery and hospital/surgeon search by postcode or location.
Surgeon profiles, verified experience, procedure volume, registry data, patient-reported outcomes and patient experience.
NHS, private insured and private self-pay pathway support.
Insurance policy details, network checks, evidence checklist, pre-authorisation workflow, queries, approval recording and funding clearance.
Referral and secretary workflow, patient communications, appointment and theatre coordination.
Patient QR/barcode identifier, secure access and check-in use cases.
Roadmap, reminders, estimated timeframes, confirmed dates and post-operative recovery tracking.
AI-assisted extraction, summarisation and workflow support, subject to safety and regulatory assessment.
3.2 Out of scope for initial MVP unless separately approved
Autonomous diagnosis, autonomous surgical eligibility decisions or autonomous urgency allocation.
Unsupervised interpretation of radiology images for diagnosis or surgical planning.
Guaranteed insurer coverage or automatic approval without insurer confirmation.
Guaranteed real-time theatre availability unless connected to a validated scheduling source.
Public display of non-published surgeon-level complication or infection rates.
Processing real patient data in a prototype or development environment without approved security, privacy and clinical governance.
3.3 Critical assumptions
The launch jurisdiction is the UK. NHS rules, private insurer policies, professional recognition, data protection, medical device regulation and interoperability must be checked for the relevant UK nation and deployment context. This document is a product specification, not legal advice or clinical guidance.

5. MediPath patient app and website requirements
5.1 Onboarding and account
Support secure registration, sign-in, account recovery and multi-factor authentication where appropriate.
Capture minimum identity/contact data needed for the intended service; support verified identity workflows appropriate to deployment.
Explain platform role, limitations, privacy notice, terms, data sharing and consent/authorisation choices in plain language.
Allow a patient to nominate a carer or representative with granular, revocable access.
Support accessibility settings, language preference, communication preference and reasonable adjustments.
5.2 Patient health profile
Data group
Fields / behaviour
Demographics
Name, date of birth, contact details, postcode, preferred contact method; collect only what is necessary.
Clinical history
Current and past conditions, previous surgery, relevant family history if clinically requested, comorbidities.
Symptoms
Affected joint, laterality, onset/duration, pain, stiffness, swelling, instability, mobility and functional impact.
Patient-reported measures
Configurable validated pain, function and quality-of-life questionnaires; instrument name, version, date and score retained.
Medication / allergy
Current medication, dose where relevant, allergy and reaction, reconciliation date.
Previous treatment
Physiotherapy, exercise, analgesia, injections, aids, other conservative care, response and dates.
Clinical documents
Referral letters, clinic letters, imaging reports, blood tests, relevant records and provenance.
Preferences
Location, accessibility, spoken language, communication requirements, appointment preferences and optional cultural/religious accommodation requests.
Questionnaires must be configurable by specialty and clinical governance. Do not treat a patient-entered answer as a verified diagnosis. Record who supplied each item and when.
5.3 Imaging and document management
Accept supported document formats and image files; define file size, malware scanning, retention and upload limits.
Support DICOM imaging and DICOMweb integration as a planned specialist capability; do not treat a screenshot/photo of an X-ray as equivalent to a full diagnostic imaging study.
Store original file, metadata, upload timestamp, uploader, source organisation, study date, body region, modality, laterality and report link where available.
Provide secure preview/download subject to role permission; preserve original source and version history.
AI may extract metadata or produce a draft summary, but must display confidence/uncertainty and allow clinician correction.
Do not allow AI-generated findings to overwrite radiologist or clinician reports.
5.4 Treatment and procedure discovery
A patient should be able to search by diagnosis, symptom, procedure or referral. Example journey: osteoarthritis → knee osteoarthritis → total knee arthroplasty / knee replacement. The interface must explain that the app does not decide whether surgery is clinically appropriate.
Support synonyms, lay terms and clinical terms, including arthroplasty and total knee replacement.
Show a plain-language procedure explanation, typical pathway stages, questions to ask the surgeon and links to authoritative patient information.
Filter providers by postcode/radius, travel preference, NHS/private/insured/self-pay pathway, insurer network, accessibility and language.
Show whether a result is a hospital, trust, clinic, consultant or independent provider, and distinguish each clearly.
5.5 Hospital and surgeon discovery
Map and list view of relevant NHS trusts, NHS hospitals and independent hospitals near a patient-selected postcode.
Allow search by hospital name, trust, consultant name, specialty and procedure.
Show network status only when confirmed by the insurer or a maintained verified provider directory.
Display distance or travel estimates only from a reliable geolocation/routing service and with clear caveats.
Provide source and last-updated date for hospital and clinician information.

Relevant procedure count per year and/or published reporting period; identify source, period and whether surgeon performed or supervised.

Financial information

Do not invent a surgeon rating or create a composite score without an approved methodology. The NJR states that its public outcomes website provides recent practice information including procedure volumes; its surgeon outcomes monitoring uses risk-adjusted revision and mortality measures, while individual surgeon revision rates are not publicly published through that website. Do not imply that unavailable NJR data is accessible or publish private internal monitoring information.
5.7 Patient testimonials
Obtain explicit permission for publication and separate consent for identifiable images/video.
Verify that the reviewer is a real patient without exposing protected health information.
Do not incentivise positive reviews or suppress legitimate negative feedback.
Moderate for abuse, personal data, advertising claims and clinical misinformation; preserve a fair process.
Display date, procedure context and a statement that individual experiences do not predict another patient's outcome.
5.8 Patient barcode / QR code
Generate a unique opaque patient token; never encode diagnosis, name, date of birth or medical record contents directly in a QR code.
Require authenticated staff session and role-based authorisation after scanning.
Support token expiry, revocation, rotation and audit logging.
Use for check-in, matching a referral to the correct patient and linking an encounter to the record; not as a substitute for identity verification.
6. MediPath insurance pathway
6.1 Insurance onboarding
Capture insurer name, policy/member number, policyholder/dependent relationship, policy dates and contact details only as necessary.
Capture patient-permitted policy documents and insurer correspondence securely.
Record whether policy cover is unknown, check requested, eligible subject to terms, excluded, moratorium/underwriting query, pre-authorisation required or confirmed by insurer.
Capture excess, co-payment, annual/episode limit, outpatient/inpatient limits, exclusions, waiting periods and any shortfall only when verified.
Never promise cover based solely on a selected insurer name or a generic benefit description.
6.2 Provider network checks
For the selected procedure and location, verify whether the consultant, hospital/facility and proposed procedure are recognised/eligible under the patient's exact policy and authorisation conditions. The same insurer may have different products, networks, fee schedules and policy terms.

6.5 Insurer integrations
Initial MVP: staff-assisted verification and secure document submission, with manual recording of insurer responses. Later: API or secure portal integration only after a contract, technical documentation, security assessment and insurer approval. Build an insurer adapter layer so rules and connection methods are configurable per payer. Do not scrape insurer portals or automate credential sharing.
Insurer directory and policy-rule versioning with effective dates.
Provider recognition verification and audit trail.
Secure submission bundle, attachment manifest and delivery receipt.
Insurer query inbox, response deadlines and resubmission history.
Approval/decline response capture with structured fields and original correspondence.
Claims/billing hand-off and reconciliation as a later phase.
7. MediWay hospital and administrator portal

Concept image 3 — MediWay hospital administration and insurance worklist dashboard.
7.1 Worklist and case management
Role-based queues for new referrals, incomplete clinical evidence, insurance checks, ready-to-submit cases, insurer queries, authorised cases, booking-ready cases and overdue tasks.
Search by patient ID, referral ID, consultant, hospital, procedure, insurer and status.
Assign case owner, priority, due date and escalation; record reason and user for every change.
Show task history and next required action, not merely a status label.
Support configurable SLA targets without implying insurer turnaround guarantees.
7.2 Referral intake and secretary workflow
Receive referral from patient, GP, consultant, e-referral integration or approved external source.
Match patient identity safely and check for duplicates.
Create a referral episode with diagnosis, procedure requested, laterality, source and date.
Allocate to consultant/secretary/triage queue.
Request missing documents and notify patient/referrer.
Arrange consultation and track attendance/outcome.
Record consultant recommendation and proposed procedure.
Open insurer funding workflow or NHS/self-pay pathway as appropriate.
Once clinical, funding and operational checks are complete, progress to pre-operative assessment and theatre scheduling.
7.3 Insurance administrator workspace
Display a structured evidence checklist and completeness indicator.
Allow upload, categorisation, versioning and validation of documents.
Generate a consistent insurer submission pack from approved templates.
Require staff confirmation before sending.
Record channel, recipient, timestamp, delivery reference and attachment list.
Track insurer queries and assign actions.
Record decision and transcribe approval terms into structured fields; require second-person verification for high-impact financial details if configured.
Block the 'financially cleared' status until all required fields and checks are complete.
7.4 Funding and financial clearance
Reconcile authorisation against the exact procedure, laterality, consultant, facility, date window and approved fee schedule.
Record known excess, co-payment, benefit limit, excluded items, shortfall and patient payment agreement.
Show quote validity and what is included/excluded (surgeon, anaesthetist, hospital, implant, diagnostics, physiotherapy, follow-up).
Flag any change to consultant, facility, procedure, implant or date that may require insurer amendment.
Separate patient consent to proceed financially from insurer approval and clinical consent.
7.5 Booking and theatre coordination
Integrate with hospital patient administration and theatre management systems where available.
Show clinic and theatre slots only from an authoritative connected source.
Maintain tentative hold, confirmed appointment, confirmed surgery, cancelled, rescheduled and completed states.
Support cancellation lists and patient opt-in for short-notice slots.
Trigger pre-operative assessment, anaesthetic review, tests and patient instructions.
Never show an estimated date as a confirmed date.

9. AI and clinical decision support
AI must be modular and purpose-limited. The first release should use AI for administrative assistance and clinician-reviewed information extraction, not autonomous clinical decision-making.
9.1 Permitted initial AI functions
Extract document metadata and draft a structured clinical summary with source citations to the uploaded document/page.
Identify missing fields or inconsistent dates, laterality, patient identifiers and procedure terminology.
Summarise symptom history and conservative treatments for clinician review.
Classify administrative workflow completeness and suggest the next non-clinical task.
Explain patient pathway stages in plain language using approved content.
Assist staff in preparing a draft insurer evidence pack, with human verification before submission.
9.2 Higher-risk AI functions requiring separate approval
Radiology image interpretation, OA grading, disease severity scoring or diagnostic suggestions.
Urgency/triage scoring or predicting time-to-treatment.
Clinical eligibility for arthroplasty, surgical risk prediction or treatment recommendation.
Surgeon ranking based on clinical outcome predictions.
These functions may constitute medical device software depending on intended purpose and claims. Before implementation, determine MHRA qualification/classification, conformity route, evidence requirements, human factors, clinical validation, post-market monitoring and applicable AI governance. Do not infer urgency from radiographic severity alone. A clinician must assess symptoms, function, examination, comorbidity, red flags, patient preference and clinical context.
9.3 Clinical urgency safeguards
AI may flag potentially urgent content for prompt human review; it must not silently assign a definitive urgency category.
Provide emergency advice and escalation pathways based on clinically approved content, not a routine waiting-list workflow.
Record model/version, input sources, output, confidence/uncertainty, human reviewer, override and rationale.
Monitor bias and performance across relevant patient groups; do not use religion, ethnicity, language, wealth or marketing value to determine clinical priority.
Allow clinician override and preserve an audit trail.

11. Architecture and integration requirements
11.1 Recommended architecture
Patient mobile app: cross-platform native or cross-platform framework; responsive patient web app.
Staff web portal: responsive web application with role-based modules.
Backend: modular API services for identity, patient record, referral, directory, insurer workflow, scheduling, notifications, document management and analytics.
Database: relational transactional store for core entities; encrypted object storage for documents and imaging; audit/event store with tamper-resistant controls.
Integration layer: adapter pattern for insurer APIs, hospital PAS/EPR, theatre scheduling, NHS referral services, identity and imaging systems.
AI service boundary: separately versioned models/services with explicit input/output contracts and human-review workflow.
Observability: monitoring, tracing, alerting, security events, audit logs and service health.
11.2 Interoperability
Use HL7 FHIR resources and implementation guides where supported by counterpart systems. Candidate mappings include Patient, Practitioner, Organization, Condition, AllergyIntolerance, MedicationStatement/MedicationRequest, Observation, DiagnosticReport, ImagingStudy, DocumentReference, ServiceRequest, Encounter, Appointment, CarePlan, Task, Coverage, Claim and ClaimResponse. Exact resource/profile selection must be confirmed against the target integration and UK implementation guidance.
Use terminology standards and controlled code sets appropriate to the data domain; validate SNOMED CT, ICD-10, OPCS-4, CCSD and other coding needs with clinical/insurer partners.
Support DICOM/DICOMweb for imaging exchange where counterpart systems permit.
Maintain source identifiers, mapping provenance and reconciliation rules.
Never assume all NHS trusts, private hospitals or insurers expose the same APIs.

12. Security, privacy and governance
Treat health information as special category personal data. Identify both an Article 6 lawful basis and an Article 9 condition; document purpose, retention and sharing.
Complete a Data Protection Impact Assessment before high-risk processing and before live patient deployment.
Apply data minimisation, privacy by design/default, purpose limitation and transparent privacy notices.
Encrypt data in transit and at rest; manage keys securely; separate production, test and development environments.
Use least-privilege role-based access, strong authentication, session controls, periodic access review and organisation-level tenancy isolation.
Maintain audit logs for viewing, editing, exporting, sharing, authorisation and administrative actions.
Define retention, deletion, legal hold, subject access, correction, portability and breach response processes.
Use synthetic/de-identified data in demos and testing; prohibit production patient data in unapproved AI tools.
Conduct supplier security due diligence, penetration testing, vulnerability management, backups, disaster recovery and incident response.
Make religion and cultural accommodation preferences optional and patient-controlled; recognise that religious belief is also special category data under UK GDPR.
13. Clinical safety and regulatory requirements
The development team must appoint/engage a suitably competent Clinical Safety Officer and maintain a clinical risk management system. Assess applicability and compliance with DCB0129 for manufacture and DCB0160 for deployment/use. The adopting healthcare organisation must perform its own deployment risk assessment. Prepare a hazard log, clinical safety management plan and clinical safety case report as appropriate.
Assess the product's status under UK medical device rules. Features that merely store, communicate or administratively organise information may be treated differently from software intended to diagnose, predict, monitor or recommend clinical treatment. The intended purpose, claims, functionality and clinical impact determine regulatory status; obtain specialist regulatory advice before release.
Also assess DTAC, accessibility, NHS information governance, UK GDPR/Data Protection Act, equality and consumer law, advertising/endorsement rules, professional registration verification, medical records retention and applicable devolved-nation requirements.
14. Commercial and marketplace requirements
Potential revenue: hospital SaaS subscription, implementation/integration fees, insurer workflow contracts and optional patient-paid services where lawful and transparent.
Do not sell clinical ranking or allow sponsored placements to distort clinical matching. Label any paid promotion distinctly and keep it separate from clinical suitability and outcome data.
Disclose conflicts of interest, referral fees and commercial relationships.
Support NHS, insured private and self-pay pathways without implying that NHS care is inferior or that private treatment is automatically clinically preferable.
Allow a provider to maintain a verified profile but distinguish self-reported claims from independently verified information.

22. Final instruction to the AI build agent
Build MediPath and MediWay as one secure, interoperable, multi-organisation healthcare workflow platform. Prioritise a reliable end-to-end insured knee replacement pathway over breadth of specialties. Ensure every status is explicit, every metric is sourced, every consequential decision is attributable to an authorised human or verified external system, and every patient-facing statement distinguishes an estimate from a confirmed clinical, financial or booking decision.
The product is not complete when a patient can find a surgeon. It is complete when the patient can safely move through the appropriate referral, clinical review, insurer or funding process, booking, treatment and recovery pathway, with the hospital team able to coordinate and audit each step.
